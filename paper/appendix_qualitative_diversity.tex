\section{Qualitative Results Across Identities}
\label{sec:appendix_identities}

The qualitative figures in the main paper show a single validation subject.
To verify that the failure mode of joint view--temporal compression and its
recovery with latent capacity are not specific to one face, we repeat the
capacity comparison of Fig.~\ref{fig:qual_capacity} on three additional
validation identities spanning gender and skin tone
(Figs.~\ref{fig:qual_identities_a}--\ref{fig:qual_identities_c}). All models,
frames, views, and inset regions follow the protocol of the main figure; only
the subject changes.

The pattern is consistent across identities. At the native latent width, the
jointly compressed model degrades precisely the articulated regions: teeth are
smeared into a single band, mouth corners are displaced, and eye detail is
lost, while static regions (forehead, cheeks, clothing) remain comparatively
clean. Widening the latent restores these regions progressively, and the best
combination recovers most of the remaining gap. The severity of the failure
scales with the amount of articulation in the clip: for the subject with a
wide-open laugh (Fig.~\ref{fig:qual_identities_a}), the native-width
reconstruction drops to 22.6\,dB on the shown frame with the mouth interior
essentially erased, whereas subjects with closed or moderately open mouths
degrade more mildly (26--27\,dB). The error maps (bottom rows) confirm that
residual error concentrates on hair boundaries and facial articulation for
every identity, i.e.\ the compression bottleneck spends its budget on exactly
the regions that matter for talking-head applications.
