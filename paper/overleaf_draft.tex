\section{Introduction}

Photo-realistic head avatars are commonly built from synchronized multi-view facial video
captured using dense camera rigs \cite{lombardi2018deep, lombardi2021mixture, cao2022authentic,
kirschstein2023nersemble, qian2024gaussianavatars}. Such setups produce large volumes of facial
video that exhibit strong redundancy along two dimensions: \emph{time}, where adjacent frames
differ by smooth facial motion, and \emph{view}, where nearby cameras observe overlapping facial
regions with shared appearance and geometry \cite{taubner2025mvp4d, shah2024mv2mae}.

Modern generative pipelines do not operate on pixels, instead latent diffusion models generate in
the compressed latent space of a Variational Auto Encoder (VAE) \cite{rombach2022high}, and video
models such as Wan \cite{wan2025wan}, CogVideoX \cite{yang2024cogvideox}, HunyuanVideo
\cite{kong2024hunyuanvideo} and Cosmos \cite{agarwal2025cosmos} rely on a causal 3D video VAE that
compresses space by 8x and time by 4x. Therefore, the tokenizer defines what the generator can
express \cite{yu2024language}. If we want to generate, edit, or interpolate multi-view facial data
with diffusion models, we first need a latent representation of multi-view video. However, no such
tokenizer exists: in recent work multi-view streams are encoded independently, which (i) ignores
the massive view redundancy and (ii) provides no architectural prior for cross-view consistency,
where each view's latent can drift independently under generation or editing.

This motivates a single jointly learned 4D latent across space, time and view. Rather than
designing a 4D architecture from scratch, we ask: can a pretrained 3D video VAE be minimally
extended to absorb the view axis? We extend the Wan 2.1 VAE \cite{wan2025wan} with
zero-initialized cross-view fusion in the encoder and view-conditioned decoding via low-rank
adapters \cite{hu2022lora, zhang2023adding}, keeping the pretrained backbone frozen so the latent
remains compatible with unconditional sampling. Through an extensive experimental study on
NeRSemble \cite{kirschstein2023nersemble} we find the following: the model reconstructs well when
either the temporal axis is compressed (4x, the native Wan setting) or the view axis is fused into
a shared latent, but combining both degrades reconstructions with artifacts, where views collapse
toward their mean and frames bleed into each other. The 16-channel latent has no spare rate for a
second compressed axis. This is consistent with the broader tokenizer literature, where
reconstruction quality under higher compression is recovered by widening the latent channel
dimension \cite{dai2023emu, chen2024deep, hacohen2024ltx}, although wider latents themselves are
known to complicate downstream generative modeling \cite{yao2025vavae}. Our extension inherits
this rate bottleneck without inheriting the fix: fusing a third axis into a channel budget
designed for one collapses views toward their mean rather than separating them.

% [added] contributions block -- CVPR readers look for this at the end of the intro
Contributions (draft):
\begin{itemize}
  \item A minimal, weight-compatible extension of a pretrained 3D video VAE to synchronized
        multi-view video: zero-init cross-view fusion in the encoder, per-view low-rank
        adapters in the decoder; identical to the pretrained model at step 0.
  \item A controlled empirical study of joint view+temporal compression at fixed latent width:
        same data, same budget, only the compressed axes vary.
  \item Two diagnostics that localize the failure (cross-view similarity for ghosting, bleed
        ratio for temporal averaging), separating it from generic reconstruction error.
  \item Evidence that the failure is a rate/capacity limit, not a fusion-design problem:
        fusion and conditioning variants barely matter, overfitting is clean, failure appears
        only under generalization.
\end{itemize}


\section{Related Work}

Although our target application is head avatar modeling from multi-view facial video, temporal
and view redundancy are studied more broadly in the video representation learning literature,
which we shortly review.

\subsection{Single-view video tokenizers}

\begin{itemize}
  \item latent diffusion \cite{rombach2022high} extended from images to videos by a) inflating
        image autoencoders with temporal layers \cite{blattmann2023align} or b) training causal
        3D tokenizers \cite{yu2024language}
  \item video foundation models converged to: causal 3D conv, 8x spatial, 4x temporal
        compression, 16-channel latent, trained with L1+LPIPS+KL+GAN objective
        \cite{esser2021taming} -- carried over from images with little change \cite{wan2025wan,
        yang2024cogvideox, kong2024hunyuanvideo, agarwal2025cosmos}, many variants
  \item tuned to compress exactly one/two axes of redundancy (temporal motion, fixed spatial
        rate) -- no view notion
  \item multi-view data passed through a standard video model is encoded stream-wise --
        redundant, not efficient
  \item our question: what happens if you add a second compressed axis to this fixed budget?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Latent capacity}

\begin{itemize}
  \item not about what redundancy a tokenizer exploits, but: how much compression a latent can
        support before quality degrades
  \item Rombach et al. \cite{rombach2022high} show this already in the original LDM ablation,
        trading downsampling factor against channel count
  \item Emu \cite{dai2023emu}: fixed 8x spatial compression, widening the latent from 4 to 16
        channels -- AE recovers small objects instead of losing them
  \item DC-AE \cite{chen2024deep}: pushes compression up to 128x, but only with the channel
        count scaled with it -- width is a necessary ingredient of the recipe
  \item LTX-Video \cite{hacohen2024ltx}: 1:192 spatiotemporal compression bought directly with a
        128-channel latent, eight times the standard video-VAE width
  \item VA-VAE (counterpoint) \cite{yao2025vavae}: wider latents reconstruct better but are
        harder for a diffusion transformer to sample from; their fix is aligning the latent with
        a frozen vision foundation model rather than widening further
  \item in short: a new axis of compression can be recovered by adding channels, at a cost on
        the generation side
  \item none of it asks what happens when the added axis is \emph{view} rather than space or
        time, on a latent that is frozen rather than trained from scratch
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Multi-view and 4D generation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item view consistency is typically solved architecturally (cross-view attention) or
        geometrically (explicit 3D)
  \item cross-view attention as default mechanism in multi-view diffusion \cite{shi2023mvdream,
        gao2024cat3d, voleti2024sv3d, zuo2024videomv}: every view attends to every other at
        generation time -- enforces consistency without compressing views into a shared
        representation
  \item MV2MAE \cite{shah2024mv2mae}: reconstructs views from synchronized inputs via masked
        autoencoding, but the objective is reconstruction, not compression; the features are not
        a latent a generative model can sample from
  \item 4D generation methods extend consistency to space/time/view but rely on iterative
        sampling or explicit geometry \cite{zhang20244diffusion, xie2024sv4d, shao2024human4dit,
        jiang2026mesh4d}, again without a compact representation
  \item explicit geometry (meshes, radiance fields, Gaussians) \cite{qian2024gaussianavatars,
        kirschstein2023nersemble}: every view is a rendering of the same structure, so
        consistency is free -- but there is no compact latent to sample; the geometry itself is
        the object being modeled, exactly what a generative model is supposed to abstract away
  \item pipelines for multi-view facial data \cite{lombardi2018deep, lombardi2021mixture} share
        these limitations: per-frame / pixel-level, no latent space
  \item 4D generative models are exactly the downstream consumers of such a latent (editing,
        interpolation, unconditional synthesis of multi-view facial video) -- a shared
        space/time/view latent is the natural intermediate representation, and it is currently
        missing
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The gap}

\begin{itemize}
  \item no prior work jointly compresses time and view into a single learned latent a downstream
        generator can sample from: video VAEs compress time, not view; multi-view methods handle
        view but without compression
  \item the latent-capacity literature suggests the fix (widen the tokenizer so the view axis
        gets its own share of the budget) -- but nobody has measured the cost of \emph{not}
        widening
  \item we run this experiment on a real pretrained backbone rather than from scratch
\end{itemize}


\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Preliminaries: Wan 2.1 Video VAE}

\begin{itemize}
  \item causal 3D convolutions, 16-channel latent, 8x spatial / 4x temporal compression
  \item chunked feature cache mechanism: frame 0 is encoded/decoded alone, then the following
        frames in chunks of 4; each temporal convolution consumes a rolling cache from the
        previous chunk; decoding mirrors this (1 latent frame in, 4 frames out, persistent
        cache) -- important later: the 4 frames of a chunk are exactly where bleeding happens
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Architecture: multi-view extension}

Input is $[B, V, C, T, H, W]$.

\subsubsection{Design decisions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item adapt a pretrained 3D VAE instead of training a 4D VAE from scratch: transfer of
        pretrained knowledge \cite{carreira2017quo, blattmann2023align}, data efficiency, and
        the latent distribution stays close to the pretrained one
  \item all new paths are zero-initialized, so the model is exactly the pretrained per-view VAE
        at step 0
  \item encoder-side cross-view fusion at the bottleneck: per-view frozen Wan encoder stems,
        then fusion at 384 channels on the 8x-downsampled grid -- tokens are cheap for
        attention, but early enough that fused information shapes the latent
  \item fusion mechanism (default): cross-view attention -- multi-head attention over all
        $V \cdot N$ tokens per time frame, RMSNorm, zero-init output projection; followed by a
        binary tree merge (pairwise concat through 2 ResBlocks) down to one stream
  \item view-conditioned decoding: the single fused latent $[16, T', H/8, W/8]$ is decoded $V$
        times by the shared frozen decoder; view identity is injected via per-view latent LoRA
        adapters (Conv3d $z \to \text{rank} \to z$, zero-init up-projection)
  \item trainability: pre-fusion per-view encoder stem frozen; bottleneck + full decoder get
        LoRA rank 32 with zero-init up-projections; new fusion modules fully trained; strided
        temporal convolutions frozen (no LoRA path exists for strided convs -- a stated
        limitation, revisited in the unfreezing ablation)
\end{itemize}

% [added] training discipline is part of the method, not just the eval protocol
\subsubsection{Training discipline}
\begin{itemize}
  \item overfit first, then generalize: every architectural change must first reconstruct a
        single sequence near-perfectly before it gets a full training run -- separates
        implementation/architecture failures from generalization behavior
  \item staged initialization (default): the joint view+temporal model warm-starts from the
        converged temporal-compression checkpoint with re-randomized view attention, i.e.\ the
        model learns the temporal axis first and the view axis second; joint training from Wan
        weights only is an ablation
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Objective}

\begin{itemize}
  \item loss: $\text{nll} = (\text{L1} + 1.5 \cdot \text{LPIPS}) / \exp(\sigma) + \sigma$ with a
        learned scalar $\sigma$, plus $10^{-6} \cdot$ KL -- the standard recipe
        \cite{rombach2022high, zheng2024opensora}
  \item optional adversarial term with three multi-view discriminator layouts (PatchGAN-style 3D
        \cite{isola2017image}): (a) views flattened into the batch, (b) views stacked on
        channels, (c) an explicit 4D discriminator with a view axis and per-view embeddings
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data and Preprocessing}

\begin{itemize}
  \item NeRSemble \cite{kirschstein2023nersemble}: 16 synchronized cameras, 73 fps, 3208x2200;
        redundant along time (smooth facial motion) and view (neighboring cameras, same face)
  \item this pipeline: select 8 frontal/upper-row cameras; temporal subsampling to 24 fps, then
        uniform selection of $T = 9$ frames
  \item per-camera color correction (as provided with the dataset tooling)
  \item center square crop; background removal with RobustVideoMatting \cite{lin2022robust},
        composited on white; bilinear resize to $\{128, 256, 512\}^2$
  \item stored as $[V, T, C, H, W]$ in $[0,1]$; trained as $[B, V, C, T, H, W]$ rescaled to
        $[-1,1]$
  \item data scales: single sequence (overfitting) $\to$ one person, all expressions $\to$ all
        participants, one expression $\to$ all participants, all expressions
  \item 10 held-out validation identities (p018, p030, p038, p085, p097, p124, p175, p226,
        p227, p240)
\end{itemize}


\section{Experiments}

Training pipeline adapted from the Open-Sora VAE trainer (multi-view data loading, per-view
gradient checkpointing); one L40S GPU per run.

% [added] overview first, so the reader (and the supervisor) sees the plan in one place
\subsection{Overview of planned experiments}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Core rate--quality matrix:} \{per-view, fused latent\} $\times$ \{temporal
        compression off, on\}, plus pretrained Wan zero-shot as floor. Single-axis compression
        should degrade gracefully -- does joint compression fall off the curve? (headline)
  \item \textbf{Fusion mechanism:} cross-attention vs.\ joint self-attention vs.\ channel-concat
        Conv3d vs.\ factorized 4D conv -- is the bottleneck the fusion design?
  \item \textbf{View-conditioned decoding:} per-view latent LoRA vs.\ learned view embedding
        vs.\ neither (negative control; expect ghosting)
  \item \textbf{View count:} $V = 2, 4, 8$ at fixed budget -- rate pressure grows with $V$
  \item \textbf{Unfreezing:} LoRA-only vs.\ unfrozen encoder vs.\ everything unfrozen -- is it
        trainability or capacity?
  \item \textbf{Initialization:} staged warm-start (default) vs.\ joint from Wan weights
  \item \textbf{Loss:} no discriminator vs.\ the three multi-view discriminator layouts;
        perceptual/KL weight sensitivity; LoRA rank
  \item \textbf{Data scale:} single sequence $\to$ one person $\to$ all people -- artifacts
        should appear only when the model must generalize
\end{itemize}
All numbers below come from reruns under one fixed protocol; per-experiment configs are pinned
in the repository.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\begin{itemize}
  \item two stages for every configuration: \emph{overfit} -- train on a single sequence,
        reconstruction must be near-perfect (gate: train PSNR $\geq 35$ held for 3 consecutive
        epochs), verifies the architecture; \emph{generalize} -- train on all participants (one
        expression), evaluate on held-out identities: encode rather than memorize
  % [added] comparability spelled out -- this is what makes the tables defensible
  \item comparability: budget is defined in optimizer updates, not epochs or wall clock;
        effective batch is fixed at 64 (micro-batch $\times$ accumulation, re-balanced
        automatically on memory limits and reported if used), so every variant sees the same
        number of updates AND the same samples; all evaluations fire at the same update steps;
        no variant is early-stopped or extended individually -- a plateau within the budget is
        the result, not a reason to train longer
  \item qualitative figures always show the same fixed participants (selected by dataset order,
        identical across all variants) -- grids are directly comparable between models
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Dataset}

\begin{itemize}
  \item train and evaluate on NeRSemble (see Sec.~3.5): $V=2$ synchronized frontal views,
        $T=9$ frames at 128x128, one expression sequence per participant
  \item why $T=9$: latent $T'=3$ = frame 0 + two 4-frame chunks, so both bleeding within chunks
        and artifacts across a chunk boundary are measurable ($T=5$ has no boundary at all)
  \item why $V=2$: the minimal view count means any capacity failure is attributable to the
        joint representation itself, not to an aggressive compression ratio; also a clean 2x
        rate factor against the per-view references; larger $V$ is an explicit ablation
  \item why 128x128: affordable controlled comparison of the full matrix on single GPUs;
        resolution is scaled separately as its own check
  \item 10 participants held out entirely for validation; all reported metrics are computed on
        these unseen identities
  \item unless stated otherwise: AdamW (lr 5e-4, constant), bf16, effective batch 64, 170
        epochs, LoRA rank 32, EMA 0.9999, no discriminator
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Metrics}

\begin{itemize}
  \item PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS \cite{zhang2018unreasonable} over the full validation set
  \item plus 3 diagnostics that localize \emph{where} information is lost:
        (i) \emph{cross-view similarity} -- mean pairwise cosine similarity between
        reconstructed views, with the GT views' similarity as reference (above the reference =
        view ghosting);
        (ii) \emph{bleed ratio} -- ratio of mean absolute inter-frame differences of the
        reconstruction to those of GT, within 4-frame chunks and across chunk boundaries
        (1 = faithful motion, $\ll 1$ = temporal bleeding); we also plot the raw per-frame
        $|\Delta|$ curves, where flat segments inside chunks make the bleeding directly visible;
        (iii) the per-frame-index error profile, exposing cold-start and chunk-boundary
        artifacts
\end{itemize}

% [added] the comparison framing -- without this the tables look like unfair baselines
\subsubsection{Reference points}
\begin{itemize}
  \item no like-for-like baseline exists (nothing else produces a joint multi-view latent), so
        per-view models are reported as \emph{reference points}, not baselines: pretrained Wan
        zero-shot, and per-view LoRA-finetuned Wan
  \item both use $V\times$ our latent rate and enforce no cross-view consistency -- all models
        are therefore placed on a rate--quality plane instead of a single-rate ranking
  \item compression ratio: $r = (V \cdot 3 \cdot T \cdot H \cdot W) /
        (V' \cdot 16 \cdot T' \cdot (H/8)(W/8))$; our configs span per-view without TC up to
        fused + TC (todo: exact numbers per config)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Main Results}

Tables and figures land here after the reruns. Expected shape of the results, based on prior
(to-be-repeated) experiments -- may change:
\begin{itemize}
  \item joint compression collapses; single-axis compression is fine (the rate--quality curve
        is the headline figure)
  \item fusion mechanism barely matters (cross-attention, joint self-attention, conv variants)
        -- fusion design is not the bottleneck
  \item view conditioning barely matters (learnable view embeddings vs.\ per-view LoRAs)
  \item loss config matters: best with no discriminator or the 4D multi-view discriminator;
        standard 3D PatchGAN gives worse colors and less sharpness -- if a discriminator is
        used it should see the view axis jointly
  \item lower perceptual and KL weights improved pixel fidelity
  \item LoRA rank ablation (expect a plateau)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Analysis: where does joint compression fail?}

\begin{itemize}
  \item generalization failure: overfitting is clean, artifacts appear only when many
        identities are trained -- the compressed representation can memorize temporal detail
        but cannot encode it for new content
  \item chunk mechanism: decoding all latent frames in a single non-causal pass removes frame-0
        and boundary artifacts -- the artifact is the cost of causality, not of temporal
        compression per se
  % [added] tie the diagnostics into the analysis
  \item diagnostics localize the failure: cross-view similarity of reconstructions rises above
        the GT level (ghosting) while bleed ratio falls below 1 within chunks (temporal
        averaging) -- both are decoder-regresses-to-the-mean signatures
  \item table of artifact-preserving interventions (non-causal decode, unfreezing, temporal
        losses, sub-frame embeddings, ...) with their effect on the diagnostics
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ablations}

\subsubsection{Fusion mechanism}
\begin{itemize}
  \item joint self-attention: one attention over the concatenated token sequence of all views,
        then concat + 2 ResBlocks
  \item channel-concat Conv3d: views stacked on channels, 1x1x1 Conv3d + GN/SiLU + two
        symmetric (non-causal) 3D residual blocks
  \item factorized 4D convolution: spatial 3x3 Conv2d, temporal 3x3x3 Conv3d, then a view-axis
        Conv3d with kernel $(V,3,3)$ compressing $V \to 1$ -- a (2+1+1)D factorization
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{View-conditioned decoding}
\begin{itemize}
  \item alternative to per-view latent LoRA adapters: view identity injected as a learned
        per-view latent embedding (nn.Embedding($V$, 16) added to $z$)
  % [added] the negative control makes "conditioning works" a measured claim
  \item negative control: no conditioning at all -- expect full ghosting; anchors the
        cross-view-similarity diagnostic
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Pre-fusion encoder LoRA}
\begin{itemize}
  \item optionally add LoRAs to the frozen per-view encoder stem -- does adapting the encoder
        before fusion help?
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Unfreezing temporal convolutions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item strided temporal convolutions have no LoRA path and are frozen in all LoRA configs --
        unfreeze them (and optionally the full encoder / full decoder); if even full unfreezing
        does not fix joint compression, the bottleneck is capacity, not trainability
\end{itemize}

% [added] was announced in the dataset bullets but had no subsection
\subsubsection{Number of views}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $V = 2, 4, 8$ at fixed budget and fixed fusion mechanism -- rate pressure on the shared
        latent grows with $V$; expect monotone degradation and earlier onset of ghosting
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Initialization strategy}
\begin{itemize}
  \item warm-start from a temporal-compression checkpoint with re-randomized view attention
        (our default) vs.\ joint training from Wan-pretrained weights only
\end{itemize}

% [added] the data-scale sweep backs the "generalization failure" claim in the analysis
\subsubsection{Training-set size}
\begin{itemize}
  \item single sequence $\to$ one person $\to$ all participants (one expression): bleed ratio
        and ghosting vs.\ dataset size -- compression only breaks when it must generalize
\end{itemize}


\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Capacity}

\begin{itemize}
  \item in our setup: the Wan latent has 16 channels per $8 \times 8 \times 4$ pixel block,
        i.e.\ $3 \cdot 8 \cdot 8 \cdot 4 / 16 = 48\times$ compression; fusing $V=2$ views
        doubles this to $96\times$; $V=4$ gives $192\times$
  \item for comparison: Emu \cite{dai2023emu} needed a widening from 4 to 16 channels just to
        hold fine detail at comparable rates; LTX-Video \cite{hacohen2024ltx} buys $192\times$
        with 128 channels
  \item our setting demands roughly 2--4$\times$ the information density of the pretrained
        latent at fixed width -- the observed failure is what rate exhaustion predicts: the
        decoder regresses to the conditional mean along whichever axis was compressed
        (ghosting = view mean, bleeding = temporal mean)
  \item two supporting observations (todo: confirm they survive the reruns!): (i) fusion and
        view-conditioning choices barely affect quality; (ii) what fails first is
        high-frequency identity detail (nuanced facial appearance, small accessories such as
        earrings) -- precisely the detail class that widening image latents from 4 to 16
        channels preserved in Emu \cite{dai2023emu}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Bottleneck: the encoder-side latent}

\begin{itemize}
  \item the decoder side works: per-view LoRA + embeddings reliably disambiguate views from a
        shared latent; conditioning mechanisms are interchangeable
  \item the information is missing, not mis-decoded: everything operating after the bottleneck
        (embeddings, residual decoders, auxiliary losses) failed to recover it -- post-hoc
        fixes cannot restore information the latent never carried
  \item so: the decoder can emit distinct views, but the fixed-width latent cannot carry them
        under joint compression
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implications and Applications}

\begin{itemize}
  \item designing a 4D tokenizer likely requires scaling latent width with the number of
        compressed axes (\cite{chen2024deep, hacohen2024ltx} suggest roughly proportional
        scaling) -- i.e.\ a 4D VAE wants 32--64 channels, which means pretraining or heavy
        finetuning, not adapters
  \item alternative: keep view identity out of the latent -- compress only shared content and
        carry view as decoder conditioning (our per-view LoRA is the adapter-scale version of
        this; a real system would train it)
  \item or asymmetric: compress time natively, keep views uncompressed but consistent via
        cross-view attention -- matching what 4D diffusion systems converged to
        \cite{xie2024sv4d, shao2024human4dit}
\end{itemize}


\section{Conclusion}

\begin{itemize}
  \item (opening sentence to write last)
  \item we extended a pretrained 3D video VAE to multi-view video with zero-initialized
        cross-view fusion and per-view low-rank decoding, preserving the pretrained latent
        distribution and unconditional sampling
  \item the study on multi-view facial video shows the fixed 16-channel latent absorbs either
        4x temporal compression or the fused view axis, but not both -- joint compression
        exhausts the latent's capacity and the decoder regresses to means, producing
        cross-view ghosting and intra-chunk temporal bleeding
  \item future 4D tokenizers: scale latent capacity with the number of compressed axes, or
        keep view identity out of the latent
\end{itemize}


\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item compressed data: 9-frame clips (rate pressure grows with $T$ -- longer clips could
        shift conclusions), 2--8 frontal views, not the full rig
  \item one backbone family (Wan 2.1); the chunked-cache specifics of the temporal path are
        Wan's, other causal video VAEs may fail differently
  % [added]
  \item adapter-scale training budget: conclusions hold for LoRA-based adaptation of a frozen
        backbone; full pretraining at 4D scale could move the capacity wall (that experiment is
        exactly what this study argues one should budget for)
  \item downstream generation untested: we evaluate the latent by reconstruction; whether a
        diffusion model samples well from the fused latent is future work
  \item single domain (faces, studio capture, white background); redundancy structure of
        in-the-wild multi-view data may differ
\end{itemize}
