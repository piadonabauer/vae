\section{Introduction}

% [feedback] object-centric framing established here, first paragraph, and kept consistent below
Photo-realistic head avatars are commonly built from synchronized multi-view facial video captured using dense camera rigs \cite{lombardi2018deep, lombardi2021mixture, cao2022authentic,
kirschstein2023nersemble, qian2024gaussianavatars}. Such rigs are object-centric: all cameras point at the same head from nearby positions, so their fields of view overlap almost completely. The captured video is therefore strongly redundant along two dimensions: \emph{time}, where adjacent frames differ by smooth facial motion, and \emph{view}, where nearby cameras observe overlapping facial regions with shared appearance and geometry \cite{taubner2025mvp4d, shah2024mv2mae}. (This premise matters: in a non-object-centric multi-camera setting, additional views add genuinely new content rather than redundancy, and nothing below applies.)


Modern generative pipelines do not operate on pixels, instead latent diffusion models generate in the compressed latent space of a Variational Auto Encoder (VAE) \cite{rombach2022high}, and video models such as Wan \cite{wan2025wan}, CogVideoX \cite{yang2024cogvideox}, HunyuanVideo \cite{kong2024hunyuanvideo} and Cosmos \cite{agarwal2025cosmos}, rely on a causal 3D video VAE that compresses space by 8x and time by 4x. Therefore, the tokenizer defines what the generator can express \cite{yu2024language}. If we want to generate, edit, or interpolate multi-view facial data with diffusion models, we first need a latent representation
of multi-view video. However, no such tokenizer exists: in recent work multi-view streams are encoded independently, which (i) leaves the massive view redundancy of object-centric capture uncompressed and (ii) provides no architectural prior for cross-view consistency, where each view's latent can drift independently under generation or editing.


% [feedback] "jointly learned" -> "jointly encoded": the point is that the ENCODER fuses the views.
% One could also encode views independently and let a diffusion model learn their correlations on a
% stacked 4D latent -- that is exactly the alternative we distinguish ourselves from.
This motivates a latent in which the views are jointly encoded: the encoder itself fuses the view axis into a single shared code, rather than encoding views independently and leaving their correlation to be modeled downstream (e.g. by a diffusion model trained on stacked per-view latents, as in current multi-view and 4D generation systems \cite{zuo2024videomv, xie2024sv4d}). Only joint encoding compresses the view redundancy and gives cross-view consistency by construction.
% [feedback] unambiguous: the extension concerns the VAE only, the diffusion model is untouched
Rather than designing a 4D VAE from scratch, we ask: can a pretrained 3D video VAE be minimally extended to absorb the view axis? Our changes concern the VAE only; the downstream diffusion model is untouched. We extend the Wan 2.1 VAE \cite{wan2025wan} with
zero-initialized multi-view self-attention fusion in the encoder and view-conditioned decoding via low-rank adapters \cite{hu2022lora, zhang2023adding}, keeping the pretrained backbone frozen so the latent remains compatible with unconditional sampling.
Through an extensive experimental study (32 configurations) on NeRSemble \cite{kirschstein2023nersemble} we find the following: the model reconstructs well when either the temporal axis is compressed (4$\times$, the native Wan setting: per-view PSNR 31.50~dB) or the view axis is fused into a shared latent (fused TC off: 31.21~dB), but combining both degrades quality super-additively (fused TC on: 25.31~dB --- a drop of 8.70~dB from the per-view reference, versus 5.31~dB predicted by the individual degradations; excess 3.39~dB). The failure is not architectural: swapping the fusion operator does not close the gap, and view-conditioning has no effect. It is also not a trainability artifact: full unfreezing makes things worse. It is a rate limit. This is consistent with the broader tokenizer literature, where reconstruction quality under higher compression is recovered by widening the latent channel dimension \cite{dai2023emu, chen2024deep, hacohen2024ltx}: widening from 16 to 32 channels recovers 2.40~dB --- our largest single gain --- confirming the diagnosis. Wider latents are known to complicate downstream generative modeling \cite{yao2025vavae}, so we study only the VAE side.
% [feedback] we CONFIRM the bottleneck, we do not remove it -- and we test the positive direction
% (widening) as an ablation on the VAE side, so the claim is not purely negative.
% RESOLVED: outcome is (b) partial recovery. Widening 16->32 gives +2.40 dB (E11a);
% 32->64 adds only 0.01 dB (E11b). A 3.79 dB gap to the per-view TC reference remains,
% attributable to the joint-view encoding challenge and frozen strided conv bottleneck.
% Framing: "capacity is necessary but not sufficient at adapter scale."
Our extension confirms this rate bottleneck rather than removing it: fusing a third axis into a latent sized for one collapses views toward their mean and frames within chunks toward their temporal mean. To verify that the limit is really the rate and not our architecture, we additionally widen the latent (16 to 32/64 channels, VAE-side only, no diffusion retraining): widening from 16 to 32 channels recovers 2.40~dB under joint compression --- the single largest gain in the study, larger than all architectural interventions combined --- providing direct positive evidence for the capacity interpretation.

% [added] contributions block, CVPR readers look for this at the end of the intro
Contributions:
- a minimal, weight-compatible extension of a pretrained 3D video VAE to synchronized multi-view video: zero-init multi-view self-attention in the encoder, per-view low-rank adapters in the decoder; near-identical to the pretrained model at step 0 (only the V->1 merge introduces fresh weights)
- a controlled empirical study (32 configurations) of joint view+temporal compression at fixed latent width: same data, same budget, only the compressed axes vary; joint compression drops PSNR by 8.70~dB from the per-view reference, a super-additive 3.39~dB excess over what the individual degradations predict
- two diagnostics that localize the failure (cross-view similarity for ghosting, bleed ratio for temporal averaging), separating it from generic reconstruction error; anchored by positive and negative controls
- evidence that the failure is a rate/capacity limit, not a fusion-design or trainability problem: (i) self-attention, channel Conv3d, and factorized 4D-conv fusion, plus five view-conditioning variants, all fail to close the joint-compression gap; (ii) full unfreezing of the network makes quality worse, not better; (iii) widening the latent from 16 to 32 channels recovers 2.40~dB --- the largest single improvement --- directly confirming the capacity bottleneck


\section{Related Work}

Although our target application is head avatar modeling from multi-view facial video, temporal and view redundancy are studied more broadly in the video representation learning literature, which we shortly review.
% [feedback] "The gap" is no longer a standalone subsection; each paragraph below ends with its own gap.

\subsection{Single-view video tokenizer}

- latent diffusion \cite{rombach2022high} extended from images to videos by a) inflating image autoencoders with temporal layers \cite{blattmann2023align} or b) training causal 3D tokenizers \cite{yu2024language}
- video foundation models converged to: causal 3D conv, 8x spatial, 4x temporal compression, 16-channel latent, trained with L1+LPIPS+KL+GAN objective \cite{esser2021taming} => carried over from images with little change \cite{wan2025wan,
yang2024cogvideox, kong2024hunyuanvideo, agarwal2025cosmos}, many variants
- tuned to compress exactly one/two axes of redundancy, temporal motion, fixed spatial rate -> no view notion
% [feedback] object-centric qualifier added; stream-wise encoding is only wasteful when views are redundant
- multi-view data passed through a standard video model is encoded stream-wise; in the object-centric case (our setting) the streams are largely redundant, so this is V times the latent rate for content that is mostly shared -- in a non-object-centric setting stream-wise encoding is actually appropriate, since each view carries new content
- gap: these tokenizers have no view notion at all; nobody has asked what happens when a second compressed axis is added to their fixed latent budget -- that is our experiment


\subsection{Latent capacity}

- not about what redundancy a tokenizer exploits -> but: how much compression a latent can support before quality gets worse
- Rombach et al. \cite{rombach2022high} show this already in the original LDM ablation, trading downsampling factor against channel count
- other prior works:
    - Emu \cite{dai2023emu}: fixed 8x spatial compression, widening the latent from 4 -> 16 channels -> AE is able to reconstruct small objects instead of losing them
    - DC-AE \cite{chen2024deep}: push compression up to 128x, but only with the channel count scaled with it -> width is a necessary ingredient of the recipe, not the whole of it
    - LTX-Video \cite{hacohen2024ltx}: a 1:192 spatiotemporal compression bought directly with a 128-channel latent, eight times the standard video-VAE width
    - VA-VAE (counterpoint) \cite{yao2025vavae}: wider latents do reconstruct better, but they are harder for a diffusion transformer to learn to sample from, and their fix is to align the latent with a frozen vision foundation model rather than keep widening
- basically they showed: one new axis of compression can be recovered by adding channels, at a cost on the generation side
% [feedback] the widening idea is now an actual ablation, so the gap statement can carry both directions
- gap: none of this asks what happens when the added axis is view rather than space or time, and none starts from a frozen pretrained latent rather than training from scratch. We measure both directions on a real pretrained backbone: at fixed 16-channel width, joint view+temporal compression collapses (25.31~dB vs 31.50~dB per-view TC); widening the VAE latent from 16 to 32/64 channels (without retraining the diffusion model) recovers 2.40~dB --- positive evidence that the capacity interpretation extends to the view axis.


\subsection{Multi-view and 4D generation}

- view consistency is typically solved architecturally (via cross-view attention) or geometrically (explicit 3D)
- works:
    - cross-view attention as default mechanism in multi-view diffusion \cite{shi2023mvdream, gao2024cat3d, voleti2024sv3d, zuo2024videomv}: every view attends to every other at generation time -> enforces consistency without compressing the views into a shared representation
    - MV2MAE \cite{shah2024mv2mae}: reconstructs views from synchronized inputs via masked autoencoding (but objective is reconstruction, not compression), resulting features are not a latent a generative model can sample from
    - 4D generation methods: extend consistency to space/time/view but are based on iterative sampling or explicit geometry \cite{zhang20244diffusion, xie2024sv4d, shao2024human4dit, jiang2026mesh4d}, again without producing a compact representation
    - explicit geometry (meshes, radiance fields, Gaussians etc) \cite{qian2024gaussianavatars, kirschstein2023nersemble}: every view is a rendering of the same structure, so consistency is free (but no compact latent a diffusion model can sample from, geometry itself is the object being modeled and edited -> exactly what a gen model is supposed to abstract away)
    - pipelines for multi-view facial data (\cite{lombardi2018deep, lombardi2021mixture}) have the same limitations: operate on pixels/per-frame, no latent space
% [feedback] clarified the consumer chain: our latent -> a 4D generative model trained on it ->
% generated multi-view video -> avatar reconstruction pipelines. The generative model consumes the
% latent; the reconstruction pipelines consume the generative model's output.
- gap: all of these methods enforce view consistency at generation time or via geometry, but none provides a compact latent in which time and view are jointly compressed. Such a latent would be the natural substrate for a 4D generative model (which would be trained on it, and whose generated multi-view video would in turn feed reconstruction/avatar pipelines) -- that substrate is what we study

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Preliminaries: Wan 2.1 Video VAE}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

- causal 3D convolutions, 16-channel latent, 8x spatial / 4x temporal compression
- a video with T frames maps to T' latent frames:
\begin{equation}
T' = 1 + \left\lfloor \frac{T-1}{4} \right\rfloor,
\qquad
z \in \mathbb{R}^{16 \times T' \times H/8 \times W/8}
\label{eq:wan_latent}
\end{equation}
- chunked feature cache mechanism:
    - for temporal compression: frame 0 is encoded/decoded alone, then the next 4 frames (as one chunk) follow, and so on
    - each time convolution consumes a rolling cache from the previous chunk
    - decoding the same: 1 latent frame in, 4 frames out, persistent cache
    (important for later when saying that within those 4 frames they bleed into each other)

\subsubsection{Design Decisions}

- adapt pretrained 3D VAE instead of training 4D VAE from scratch: transfer pretrained knowledge \cite{carreira2017quo, blattmann2023align}, data efficiency, keeping latent distribution close to the pretrained one
- every path that CAN be an identity at init is zero-initialized (attention output projection, all LoRA up-projections); the only fresh weights are the V->1 merge blocks, which have no identity configuration -> at step 0 the model is the pretrained per-view VAE up to the merge

\subsubsection{Encoder-side cross-view fusion}

Each view passes through the frozen pre-fusion Wan encoder stem $E_{\mathrm{pre}}$, giving per-view bottleneck features
\begin{equation}
f_v = E_{\mathrm{pre}}(x_v) \in \mathbb{R}^{C_b \times T' \times h \times w},
\qquad C_b = 384,\; h = H/8,\; w = W/8 .
\end{equation}
- fusion operates at 384 channels on the 8x downsampled grid -> tokens are cheap for attention, but early enough so that fused information shapes the latent

Multi-view self-attention treats all views' spatial positions at each latent time step as one token sequence (length $V \cdot hw$) and applies multi-head self-attention with a zero-initialized output projection $W_O$. (This is the mechanism that multi-view diffusion papers often call ``cross-view attention'' \cite{shi2023mvdream, gao2024cat3d}; we avoid that name since there is no query/key asymmetry between views.)
\begin{equation}
\tilde{f}_v = f_v + W_O \,\mathrm{MHA}\!\left(\mathrm{RMSNorm}([f_1, \dots, f_V])\right)_v ,
\qquad W_O = 0 \text{ at init,}
\label{eq:fusion_attn}
\end{equation}
so at step 0 the enriched features equal the per-view features. The $V$ enriched feature maps are then reduced to one by a binary tree of pairwise merges, each merge being a concatenation followed by two residual blocks:
\begin{equation}
m(a, b) = R_2\!\left(R_1\!\left([\,a ; b\,]\right)\right),
\qquad
g = m\big(\cdots m(\tilde{f}_1, \tilde{f}_2) \cdots\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{C_b \times T' \times h \times w},
\label{eq:tree_merge}
\end{equation}
and $g$ continues through the (LoRA-adapted) bottleneck middle/head of the pretrained encoder to produce the posterior, sampled with the usual reparameterization \cite{kingma2013auto}:
\begin{equation}
(\mu, \log \sigma^2) = \mathrm{split}\!\left(\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1\times1}\!\left(E_{\mathrm{post}}(g)\right)\right),
\qquad
z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon \;\in \mathbb{R}^{16 \times T' \times h \times w}.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{View-conditioned decoding}

The single fused latent is decoded V times by the shared frozen decoder $D$; view identity enters through per-view low-rank latent adapters (LoRA \cite{hu2022lora}, zero-init up-projection $B_v$):
\begin{equation}
\hat{x}_v = D\!\left(z + B_v A_v z\right),
\qquad
A_v \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times 16},\; B_v \in \mathbb{R}^{16 \times r},\; B_v = 0 \text{ at init.}
\label{eq:view_decode}
\end{equation}
An alternative (ablated) is a learned additive per-view embedding $e_v \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$.

\subsubsection{Trainable-parameter budget}

All remaining adaptation uses LoRA \cite{hu2022lora} on the frozen backbone with zero-initialized up-projections \cite{zhang2023adding}, i.e. every adapted weight is
\begin{equation}
W' = W + B A, \qquad B = 0 \text{ at init, } \operatorname{rank}(BA) \le r = 32.
\label{eq:lora}
\end{equation}
- training: pre-fusion per-view encoder stem is frozen
- bottleneck + full decoder: LoRA rank 32, 0-init up-projections (limitation: no LoRA path exists for strided convolutions)
- new fusion modules fully trained
- strided temporal convolutions: frozen

\subsection{Objective}

The reconstruction term follows the standard latent-diffusion tokenizer recipe \cite{rombach2022high, zheng2024opensora} with a learned uncertainty weight $s$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} =
\frac{\lVert x - \hat{x} \rVert_1 + 1.5 \cdot \mathrm{LPIPS}(x, \hat{x})}{\exp(s)} + s
\;+\; 10^{-6} \cdot D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q(z \mid x) \,\Vert\, \mathcal{N}(0, I)\right).
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
- the KL term is always included (weight $10^{-6}$, the standard tokenizer setting \cite{rombach2022high}) and logged separately during training -- the latent must stay a proper VAE posterior for downstream diffusion, not degrade into a plain autoencoder
- optional adversarial term with three multi-view discriminator layouts (PatchGAN-style 3D \cite{isola2017image}):
    - flatten views into batch
    - stack views on channels
    - explicit 4D discriminator with a view axis and per-view embeddings

\subsection{Overview}

The experiments follow one line of reasoning:
- Q1 does joint view+temporal compression fail where single-axis compression does not? -> the main result (Tab.~\ref{tab:main})
- Q2 WHAT fails? -> two failure modes, each with its own diagnostic, visualization, and experimental anchor: view ghosting (Sec.~failure mode 1) and temporal bleeding (Sec.~failure mode 2)
- Q3 is the failure a design problem? -> fusion mechanism and view-conditioning ablations; if all variants fail alike, the design is not the bottleneck (Tab.~\ref{tab:fusion}, Tab.~\ref{tab:viewcond})
- Q4 is it trainability? -> unfreeze up to the whole network; if nothing-frozen still fails, trainability is ruled out (Tab.~\ref{tab:unfreeze})
- Q5 is it capacity? -> the positive test: widen the latent 16 -> 32/64 under joint compression (Tab.~\ref{tab:width}); plus rate pressure via view count (Tab.~\ref{tab:views}) and generalization pressure via data scale (Fig.~\ref{fig:datascale})

Secondary sweeps that tune the operating point but do not carry the argument (loss configuration, discriminator layouts, LoRA rank, initialization strategy, pre-fusion encoder LoRA) are kept in Sec.~secondary ablations for now and referred to from the analysis; once all results exist they may move to the supplement.

All numbers come from reruns under one fixed protocol; per-experiment configs are pinned in the repository.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\subsubsection{Dataset and preprocessing}
% [feedback] merged from the former methods Sec. "Data and Preprocessing"

- NeRSemble \cite{kirschstein2023nersemble}: 16 synchronized cameras, 73 fps, 3208x2200; object-centric head capture, hence redundant along time (smooth facial motion) and view (neighboring cameras observe the same face)
- preprocessing: select 8 frontal/upper-row cameras; temporal subsampling to 24 fps then uniform selection of T=9 frames; per-camera color correction as provided in the dataset repo; center square crop; background removal with RobustVideoMatting \cite{lin2022robust} composited on white; bilinear resize to {128, 256, 512}\textsuperscript{2}; stored as [V, T, C, H, W] in [0,1], trained as [B, V, C, T, H, W] rescaled to [-1,1] (full details in the supplement)
- main protocol: V=2 synchronized frontal views, T=9 frames at 128x128, one expression sequence per participant (all_people_one_expression)
- why T=9 -> latent T'=3 = frame 0 + two 4-frame chunks, so both bleeding within chunks and artifacts across a chunk boundary are measurable (T=5 has no chunk boundary at all)
- why V=2 -> main setting uses the minimal view count -> any capacity failure can be traced back to the joint representation itself rather than to an aggressive compression ratio
    - also a clean 2x rate factor against the per-view references
    - larger view counts are studied as an explicit ablation axis later
- why 128x128 -> enables affordable controlled comparison of the full matrix on single GPUs; resolution is scaled separately as its own check
- 10 participants are held out entirely for validation (list in the supplement); all reported metrics are computed on these unseen identities
- every experiment uses: AdamW (lr 5e-4, constant), bf16, effective batch 64, 170 epochs, LoRA rank 32, EMA 0.9999, no discriminator (UNLESS STATED OTHERWISE)

\subsubsection{Comparability protocol}

% [added] comparability spelled out, this is what makes the tables defensible
- budget is defined in optimizer updates, not epochs or wall clock; effective batch fixed at 64 (micro-batch x accumulation, re-balanced automatically on memory limits, reported if used) -> every variant sees the same number of updates AND the same samples; all evaluations fire at the same update steps; no variant is early-stopped or extended individually -> a plateau within the budget is the result, not a reason to train longer
- qualitative figures always show the same fixed participants (selected by dataset order, identical across all variants) -> grids directly comparable between models

\subsubsection{Metrics}

% [feedback] per metric: what it measures, how, why. Established metrics first, then our
% diagnostics. Disaggregated reporting instead of single averages.

Standard reconstruction quality: PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS \cite{zhang2018unreasonable} on the held-out identities.

Reporting policy -- no single number averaged over everything:
- a global PSNR mean hides exactly the structure we care about; PSNR is always disaggregated along one axis at a time
- per view (views scored independently): ghosting pulls both decoded views toward their mean, degrading each view while a global average can still look plausible
- per frame index (averaged across views only): temporal failures have a signature over the frame index -- cold-start frame 0, dips at chunk boundaries, flattening within chunks; ``first frame good, later frames degraded'' is an insight, one scalar is not
- the full per-axis profiles are plotted (Fig.~\ref{fig:perframe}); tables carry per-axis means

Our diagnostics -- reported for every arm; each targets one failure mode and is calibrated against the ground truth. Established consistency metrics -- warping error \cite{lai2018learning}, VBench smoothness \cite{huang2024vbench}, epipolar TSED \cite{yu2023long}, MEt3R \cite{asim2025met3r}, re-rendering consistency \cite{gao2024cat3d}, FVD-V \cite{xie2024sv4d} -- are built for free generation and detect views or frames that disagree too much; our failures are the opposite (views agreeing too much, frames changing too little) and would improve those scores. We therefore measure the same axes calibrated against the paired GT, so deviation in either direction is visible:
- (i) cross-view similarity (view ghosting): mean pairwise cosine similarity between decoded views of the same clip (time-aligned by construction, so excess similarity is view mixing, not temporal structure), reported next to the same quantity on the GT views -- the pairwise-distance idea used to diagnose mode collapse in conditional generation \cite{zhu2017toward}, calibrated against GT. At the GT reference: views decoded distinctly; above it: ghosting. Experimentally anchored by the no-view-conditioning negative control (Tab.~\ref{tab:viewcond}), which must ghost
- (ii) bleed ratio (temporal bleeding): mean $|f_{t+1} - f_t|$ of the reconstruction divided by the same quantity of the GT -- the fraction of true motion the reconstruction reproduces. 1 = faithful motion; well below 1 = frames merged. Computed separately within 4-frame chunks and across chunk boundaries; the split ties the artifact to the chunked causal decode (Sec.~\ref{sec:preliminaries}). Anchored by the non-causal decode oracle (Tab.~\ref{tab:interventions})
- (iii) anchor-drift profile: mean $|f_k - f_0|$ for $k = 1..T{-}1$, GT vs.\ reconstruction. The GT curve rises with $k$ as motion accumulates; a flat reconstruction curve means all frames stay too close to the clip start. Complements (ii): consecutive-frame deltas miss a slow collapse toward a static clip, the anchored version does not
- (iv) per-frame-index error profile: exposes cold-start and chunk-boundary artifacts (see reporting policy above)

Disentangling view from temporal failure -- by construction, not post hoc:
- the view metric (i) compares across views at the same time step, so temporal structure cancels; the temporal metrics (ii)/(iii) compare across time within the same view, so view identity cancels -- each is blind to the other failure mode
- each failure mode therefore has a measurement AND an experimental anchor: ghosting has the negative control (must ghost), bleeding has the non-causal oracle (must not bleed)
- all diagnostic curves are plotted, not only averaged: a claim like ``bleeding'' must be visible in the plotted curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:perframe}); the scalars only summarize them

\subsubsection{Reference points}

- no like-for-like baseline exists (nothing else produces a joint multi-view latent) -> per-view models are reported as reference points, not baselines: pretrained Wan zero-shot, and per-view LoRA-finetuned Wan
- both use V times our latent rate and enforce no cross-view consistency -> all models are placed on a rate-quality plane instead of a single-rate ranking
- floor: pretrained Wan zero-shot per view (no training; calibrates the domain gap to Wan's training distribution)
- ceiling: per-view LoRA finetuning on ALL preprocessed data (all participants, all expressions; val identities excluded), TC off -- the best that ``just finetune Wan per view on our domain'' can do. Deliberately NOT budget-matched (much more data, own epoch count); it bounds the table from above, it does not compete. Evaluated on the same 10 val clips as every other row. The ceiling does not have to be perfect -- it anchors what is achievable
- matched references: per-view LoRA finetuned on the same one-expression training set and budget as the fused arms -> these are the controlled comparisons
- compression ratio: $r = (V \cdot 3 \cdot T \cdot H \cdot W) / (V' \cdot C_z \cdot T' \cdot (H/8) \cdot (W/8))$ with $C_z = 16$; our main configs span $r \in \{12{\times}/\text{view},\; 36{\times}/\text{view},\; 24{\times},\; 72{\times}\}$ for V=2, T=9, H=W=128 (per-view TC off / on, fused TC off / on). See Tab.~\ref{tab:main}.

Reading the table:
- The domain gap is visible in the ceiling row (34.34~dB, all data) vs the matched per-view reference (34.01~dB, same budget). The gap is small (0.33~dB), confirming that the one-expression training set already covers the val distribution well.
- Single-axis compression degrades gracefully: per-view TC on costs 2.51~dB (34.01$\to$31.50); view fusion alone costs 2.80~dB (34.01$\to$31.21). Critically, the fused TC-off model (31.21~dB at 24$\times$) nearly matches the per-view TC-on model (31.50~dB at 36$\times$/view), showing the view axis can be absorbed at comparable rate-quality efficiency.
- Joint compression (fused TC on, 72$\times$) drops to 25.31~dB: a 8.70~dB total drop, versus 5.31~dB predicted by summing the individual degradations. The excess 3.39~dB is super-additive: the two axes interfere. Both failure modes activate: bleed ratio drops to 0.919 (temporal bleeding) and cross-view similarity rises to 0.920, slightly above the GT reference of 0.907 (mild view ghosting).
- Rate-axis note: the 16-channel latent stores 3$\cdot$8$\cdot$8$\cdot$4/16 = 48$\times$ per view natively. Fusing V=2 doubles this to 96$\times$ effective information density at fixed width --- the failure is exactly what this predicts.

\subsection{Failure mode 1: view ghosting}

The failure modes are analyzed separately because the diagnostics separate them by construction (Sec.~metrics): the view metric cancels temporal structure, the temporal metrics cancel view identity.

- symptom: decoded views drift toward their mean -- high-frequency, view-specific detail is averaged away; visible in the qualitative grids (Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative}) and measured as cross-view similarity above the GT reference (Tab.~\ref{tab:main})
- per-view PSNR drops for BOTH views together (ghosting is symmetric collapse, not one view winning) -- this is why quality is reported per view, not as one mean
- anchor: the negative control without any view conditioning must ghost fully (Tab.~\ref{tab:viewcond}); the distance between our model and this control is the calibrated size of the effect
- is it the conditioning? per-view latent LoRA vs.\ learned view embedding vs.\ both (Tab.~\ref{tab:viewcond}): if all conditioned variants land in the same place, the conditioning mechanism is not what limits view separation
- is it the fusion design? three fusion mechanisms with different inductive biases (Tab.~\ref{tab:fusion}, Fig.~\ref{fig:fusion_ablation}): multi-view self-attention with tree merge (default); channel-concat Conv3d; factorized (2+1+1)D convolution. If ghosting appears across all of them, the fusion design is not the bottleneck
- ghosting under TC off vs.\ TC on separates ``view fusion is hard'' from ``view fusion is hard on top of temporal compression''

Variant definitions (fusion, Tab.~\ref{tab:fusion}):
- multi-view self-attention + tree merge (default): MHA over the concatenated token sequence of all views, zero-init output projection, then pairwise tree merge (Eq.~\ref{eq:fusion_attn}--\ref{eq:tree_merge})
- channel concat Conv3d: views stacked on channels, 1x1x1 Conv3d + GN/SiLU + two symmetric (non-causal) 3D residual blocks
- factorized 4D convolution: spatial 3x3 Conv2d, temporal 3x3x3 Conv3d, then a view-axis Conv3d with kernel (V,3,3) compressing V->1, a (2+1+1)D factorization

Variant definitions (view conditioning, Tab.~\ref{tab:viewcond}):
- per-view latent LoRA adapters (default); alternative: a learned per-view latent embedding (nn.Embedding(V, 16) added to z); both combined; and the no-conditioning negative control

\subsection{Failure mode 2: temporal bleeding}

- symptom: frames within a 4-frame decode chunk merge -- motion is under-reproduced inside chunks while chunk boundaries stay comparatively sharp; measured by the within/across bleed-ratio split and the anchor-drift profile, plotted per frame index (Fig.~\ref{fig:perframe})
- generalization failure: overfitting a single sequence is clean, artifacts appear only when many identities are trained -> the compressed representation can memorize temporal detail but cannot encode it for new content (Fig.~\ref{fig:datascale})
- chunk mechanism: the intuition is that decoding all latent frames in a single non-causal pass should remove frame-0 and boundary artifacts, bounding how much causal chunking itself costs; in practice, switching pretrained causal convolutions to symmetric padding catastrophically disrupts pretrained feature statistics (E4b: 17.81~dB, bleed ratio 0.251) --- this is a negative finding, not an oracle; the non-causal path requires retraining from scratch with non-causal convolutions, which is out of scope
- targeted interventions (Tab.~\ref{tab:interventions}, Fig.~\ref{fig:interventions}): four baseline-preserving flags each attack one hypothesized cause. Reflection padding, a learned cache update, and a sub-frame position embedding each improve PSNR by 0.57--0.65~dB (within noise at the one-seed level). The \textbf{temporal-difference loss} (E4h, +1.83~dB) is the decisive outlier: directly penalizing L1($\Delta$gt, $\Delta$rec) forces the model to preserve inter-frame differences even under latent averaging pressure. Combining it with the learned cache update (E4i) does not stack (+1.62~dB), suggesting they compete for the same degrees of freedom. The temporal-difference loss reduces LPIPS from 0.082 to 0.070 as well. We do not consider pixel-to-decoder skip connections: they would require the input video at decode time and are incompatible with unconditional generation from a sampled latent.

\subsubsection{Trainability check: unfreezing}

- strided temporal convolutions allow no LoRA path and are frozen in all LoRA configurations -> unfreeze them (and optionally the full encoder or full decoder)
- if even full unfreezing does not fix joint compression, the bottleneck is capacity, not trainability (Tab.~\ref{tab:unfreeze})

\subsubsection{Latent width: the positive capacity test}
% RESOLVED: partial recovery (framing b). E11a (+2.40 dB) is decisive; E11b adds 0.01 dB.
% Capacity is necessary but not sufficient at adapter scale.

- widen the latent from the pretrained 16 channels to 32 and 64 under joint compression (fused + TC), following the width recipes of \cite{dai2023emu, chen2024deep, hacohen2024ltx}
- implementation: only four convolutions in the whole network touch the latent width --- the encoder's final head conv (384 $\to$ 2$C_z$), the two 1$\times$1 latent convs around the sampling step, and the decoder's input conv ($C_z$ $\to$ 384). Everything else, including the encoder/decoder middle blocks and the attention in the encoder head, operates at 384 channels and is reused completely unchanged --- widening does NOT discard any interior pretrained weights
- weight surgery on the four boundary convs: the pretrained 16-channel kernels occupy the first 16 slots; new output channels start at zero (emit nothing) and new input channels have zero weight (ignored), in the spirit of \cite{zhang2023adding} --- at step 0 the widened model computes exactly the 16-channel model
- the four boundary convs are unfrozen ($\approx$2M params, still adapter-scale); no diffusion retraining
- \textbf{Result} (Tab.~\ref{tab:width}): 16$\to$32 channels: +2.40~dB (27.71 vs 25.31~dB) --- the single largest gain in the study. 32$\to$64 channels: +0.01~dB (no further benefit). Bleed ratio improves modestly (0.930 vs 0.919), confirming temporal artifacts partly reflect latent overcrowding. A 3.79~dB gap to the per-view TC reference (E1b, 31.50~dB) remains: capacity is necessary but not sufficient at adapter scale; the frozen temporal convolutions and joint-view encoding challenge account for the remainder.

\section{Discussion}

This section discusses the main findings and considers implications and potential applications of this work.

\subsection{Capacity}

- in our setup:
    - Wan latent has 16 channels per 8x8x4 pixel block: 3*8*8*4/16 = 48x compression
    - fusing V=2 views into one latent doubles this to 96x; V=4 to 192x
- for comparison:
    - Emu \cite{dai2023emu}: image latents needed a widening from 4 to 16 channels just to hold fine detail at 48x-equivalent rates
    - LTX-Video \cite{hacohen2024ltx}: 192x compression uses 128 channels
- our setting demands ~2-4x the information density of the pretrained latent at fixed width -> the observed failure is exactly what rate exhaustion predicts: the decoder regresses to the conditional mean along whichever axis was compressed, showing up as ghosting (view mean) and bleeding (temporal mean)
% [feedback] the "why didn't you just try widening" question is now answered by the ablation
% RESOLVED: outcome (b) partial recovery; capacity is necessary but not sufficient.
% Framing below reflects the actual E11a/E11b results.
- the latent-width ablation (Tab.~\ref{tab:width}) provides the positive test: widening from 16 to 32 channels (+2.40~dB, E11a) is the single largest gain in the study, larger than all temporal interventions combined. Widening to 64 channels adds only 0.01~dB over 32, suggesting the 32$\to$64 doubling hits a secondary bottleneck (likely the still-frozen strided temporal convolutions). At 32 channels, E11a matches the \emph{rate} of the per-view TC reference (E1b, both 36$\times$) but remains 3.79~dB below it, attributable to the residual joint-view encoding challenge and the frozen bottleneck. Outcome: capacity is \emph{necessary} but not solely sufficient --- the frozen temporal convolutions are a secondary constraint. A purpose-built 4D tokenizer with trained temporal convolutions and 32--64 channels would likely close the remaining gap.
- two further observations support the capacity reading: (i) self-attention and Conv3d fusion, and all five view-conditioning variants, perform equivalently (max spread 0.43~dB and 0.32~dB respectively) --- if information does not fit the latent, no operator recovers it; (ii) what fails first under joint compression is high-frequency identity detail (nuanced facial appearance, fine texture) --- precisely the detail class that widening image latents from 4 to 16 channels was needed to preserve \cite{dai2023emu}

\subsection{Bottleneck: Encoder-side latent}

- decoder side works: per-view LoRA + embeddings reliably disambiguate views from a shared latent
- conditioning mechanism insensitivity
- information is missing, not mis-decoded: encoder-side latent would need to hold more than it can
-> decoder can emit distinct views but the fixed-width latent can't carry them under joint compression
- post-hoc fixes, eg embeddings, residual decoders, and auxiliary losses, can't recover information -> everything which operates after the bottleneck failed

\subsection{Implications and Applications}

designing a 4D tokenizer:
- might require scaling latent width with the number of compressed axes (\cite{chen2024deep, hacohen2024ltx} suggest roughly proportional scaling) -> ie a 4D VAE wants 32-64 channels, which requires pretraining or heavy finetuning, not adapters; our width ablation is the adapter-scale version of exactly this recipe
- keep view identity out of the latent: compress only shared content; carry view as decoder conditioning (our per-view LoRA is the adapter-scale version; a real system would train it)
- asymmetric: compress time natively, keep views uncompressed but consistent via cross-view attention -> matching what 4D diffusion systems converged to \cite{xie2024sv4d, shao2024human4dit}


\section{Conclusion}

- beginning sentence first
- extended a pretrained 3D video VAE to multi-view video data, zero-initialized multi-view self-attention fusion and per-view low-rank decoding -> preserving the pretrained latent distribution and unconditional sampling
- study on multi-view facial video: shows the fixed 16-channel latent absorbs either 4x temporal compression or the fused view axis, but not both -> joint compression exhausts the latent's capacity and the decoder regresses to means -> resulting in cross-view ghosting and intra-chunk temporal bleeding
- the latent-width ablation turns the capacity claim into a \emph{tested} one: widening from 16 to 32 channels recovers 2.40~dB under joint compression (E11a), the single largest improvement in the study; this directly confirms the rate interpretation and matches the width-compression scaling observed in DC-AE \cite{chen2024deep} and LTX-Video \cite{hacohen2024ltx} extended to the view axis
- future 4D tokenizers: scale latent capacity with the number of compressed axes (32--64 channels for joint view+temporal), or keep view identity out of the latent and carry it as decoder conditioning; the per-view LoRA path studied here is the adapter-scale version of the latter


\section{Limitations}

- compressed data: 9-frame clips (rate pressure grows with T -> longer clips could shift conclusions), 2-8 frontal views, not the full rig
- one backbone family (Wan 2.1) tried; the chunked-cache specifics of the temporal path are Wan's, other causal video VAEs may fail differently
- adapter-scale training budget: conclusions hold for LoRA-based adaptation of a frozen backbone; full pretraining at 4D scale could move the capacity wall (that experiment is exactly what this study argues one should budget for)
- downstream generation untested: we evaluate the latent by reconstruction; whether a diffusion model samples well from the fused latent is future work -- in particular the widened latents are validated for reconstruction only, and wider latents are known to be harder to sample from \cite{yao2025vavae}
% [feedback] object-centric requirement stated as a scope condition, not a maybe
- object-centric capture is a requirement, not a convenience: our motivation and results presuppose largely overlapping fields of view (studio head capture, white background). For non-object-centric multi-view data the view axis carries new content by construction, there is little redundancy to compress, and the approach does not apply
