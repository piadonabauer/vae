\section{Introduction}

Photo-realistic head avatars are commonly built from synchronized multi-view facial video captured using dense camera rigs \cite{lombardi2018deep, lombardi2021mixture, cao2022authentic,
kirschstein2023nersemble, qian2024gaussianavatars}. Such setups produce large volumes of facial video that exhibit strong redundancy along two dimensions: \emph{time}, where adjacent frames differ by smooth facial motion, and \emph{view}, where nearby cameras observe overlapping facial regions with shared appearance and geometry \cite{taubner2025mvp4d, shah2024mv2mae}.


Modern generative pipelines do not operate on pixels, instead latent diffusion models generate in the compressed latent space of a Variational Auto Encoder (VAE) \cite{rombach2022high}, and video models such as Wan \cite{wan2025wan}, CogVideoX \cite{yang2024cogvideox}, HunyuanVideo \cite{kong2024hunyuanvideo} and Cosmos \cite{agarwal2025cosmos}, rely on a causal 3D video VAE that compresses space by 8x and time by 4x. Therefore, the tokenizer defines what the generator can express \cite{yu2024language}. If we want to generate, edit, or interpolate multi-view facial data with diffusion models, we first need a latent representation
of multi-view video. However, no such tokenizer exists: in recent work multi-view streams are encoded independently, which (i) ignores the massive view redundancy and (ii) provides no architectural prior for cross-view consistency, where each view's latent can drift independently under generation or editing.


This motivates a single jointly learned 4D latent across space, time and view. Rather than designing a 4D architecture from scratch, we ask: can a pretrained 3D video VAE be minimally extended to absorb the view axis? We extend the Wan 2.1 VAE \cite{wan2025wan} with
zero-initialized cross-view fusion in the encoder and view-conditioned decoding via low-rank adapters \cite{hu2022lora, zhang2023adding}, keeping the pretrained backbone frozen so the latent remains compatible with unconditional sampling. Through an extensive experimental study on NeRSemble \cite{kirschstein2023nersemble} we find the following: the model reconstructs well when either the temporal axis is compressed (4x, the native Wan setting) or the view axis is fused into a shared latent, but combining both degrades reconstructions with artifacts, where views collapse toward their mean and bleed into each others views. The 16-channel latent has no spare rate for a second compressed axis. This is consistent with the broader tokenizer literature, where reconstruction quality under higher compression is recovered by widening the latent channel dimension \cite{dai2023emu, chen2024deep, hacohen2024ltx}, although wider latents themselves are known to complicate downstream generative modeling \cite{yao2025vavae}. Our extension inherits this rate bottleneck without inheriting the fix: fusing a third axis into a channel budget designed for one collapses views toward their mean rather than separating them.

% [added] contributions block, CVPR readers look for this at the end of the intro
Contributions:
- a minimal, weight-compatible extension of a pretrained 3D video VAE to synchronized multi-view video: zero-init cross-view fusion in the encoder, per-view low-rank adapters in the decoder; identical to the pretrained model at step 0
- a controlled empirical study of joint view+temporal compression at fixed latent width: same data, same budget, only the compressed axes vary
- two diagnostics that localize the failure (cross-view similarity for ghosting, bleed ratio for temporal averaging), separating it from generic reconstruction error
- evidence that the failure is a rate/capacity limit, not a fusion-design problem: fusion and conditioning variants barely matter, overfitting is clean, failure appears only under generalization


\section{Related Work}

Although our target application is head avatar modeling from multi-view facial video, temporal and view redundancy are studied more broadly in the video representation learning literature, which we shortly review.

\subsection{Single-view video tokenizer}

- latent diffusion \cite{rombach2022high} extended from images to videos by a) inflating image autoencoders with temporal layers \cite{blattmann2023align} or b) training causal 3D tokenizers \cite{yu2024language}
- video foundation models converged to: causal 3D conv, 8x spatial, 4x temporal compression, 16-channel latent, trained with L1+LPIPS+KL+GAN objective \cite{esser2021taming} => carried over from images with little change \cite{wan2025wan,
yang2024cogvideox, kong2024hunyuanvideo, agarwal2025cosmos}, many variants
- tuned to compress exactly one/two axes of redundancy, temporal motion, fixed spatial rate -> no view notion
- multi-view data passed through a standard video model is encoded stream-wise -> redundant, not efficient
- what happens if you add a second compressed axis to this fixed budget?


\subsection{Latent capacity}

- not about what redundancy a tokenizer exploits -> but: how much compression a latent can support before quality gets worse
- Rombach et al. \cite{rombach2022high} show this already in the original LDM ablation, trading downsampling factor against channel count
- other prior works:
    - Emu \cite{dai2023emu}: fixed 8x spatial compression, widening the latent from 4 -> 16 channels -> AE is able to reconstruct small objects instead of losing them
    - DC-AE \cite{chen2024deep}: push compression up to 128x, but only with the channel count scaled with it -> width is a necessary ingredient of the recipe, not the whole of it
    - LTX-Video \cite{hacohen2024ltx}: a 1:192 spatiotemporal compression bought directly with a 128-channel latent, eight times the standard video-VAE width
    - VA-VAE (counterpoint) \cite{yao2025vavae}: wider latents do reconstruct better, but they are harder for a diffusion transformer to learn to sample from, and their fix is to align the latent with a frozen vision foundation model rather than keep widening
- basically they showed: one new axis of compression can be recovered by adding channels, at a cost on the generation side
- none of it asks what happens when the axis being added is view rather than space or time, onto a latent that is frozen rather than trained from scratch


\subsection{Multi-view and 4D generation}

- view consistency is typically solved architecturally (via cross-view attention) or geometrically (explicit 3D)
- works:
    - cross-view attention as default mechanism in multi-view diffusion \cite{shi2023mvdream, gao2024cat3d, voleti2024sv3d, zuo2024videomv}: every view attends to every other at generation time -> enforces consistency without compressing the views into a shared representation
    - MV2MAE \cite{shah2024mv2mae}: reconstructs views from synchronized inputs via masked autoencoding (but objective is reconstruction, not compression), resulting features are not a latent a generative model can sample from
    - 4D generation methods: extend consistency to space/time/view but are based on iterative sampling or explicit geometry \cite{zhang20244diffusion, xie2024sv4d, shao2024human4dit, jiang2026mesh4d}, again without producing a compact representation
    - explicit geometry (meshes, radiance fields, Gaussians etc) \cite{qian2024gaussianavatars, kirschstein2023nersemble}: every view is a rendering of the same structure, so consistency is free (but no compact latent a diffusion model can sample from, geometry itself is the object being modeled and edited -> exactly what a gen model is supposed to abstract away)
- pipelines for multi-view facial data (\cite{lombardi2018deep, lombardi2021mixture}) have the same limitations: operate on pixels/per-frame, no latent space
<-> 4D generative models are exactly the downstream consumers for such a latent -> for editing, interpolation, unconditional synthesis of multi-view facial video => a shared space/time/view latent as natural intermediate representation, not provided so far


\subsection{The gap}

- no prior work jointly compresses time and view into a single learned latent where a downstream generator can sample from
    - video VAEs compress time, not view
    - multi-view methods handle view but without compression
- idea following the latent-capacity literature above: widen the tokenizer enough to give the view axis its own share of the budget
    % [added] one line sharpening the gap
    - but nobody has measured the cost of NOT widening, i.e. what actually breaks and how
- we test this on a real pretrained backbone rather than from scratch


\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Preliminaries: Wan 2.1 Video VAE}

- causal 3D convolutions, 16-channel latent, 8x spatial / 4x temporal compression
- chunked feature cache mechanism:
    - for temporal compression: frame 0 is encoded/decoded alone, then the next 4 frames (as one chunk) follow, and so on
    - each time convolution consumes a rolling cache from the previous chunk
    - decoding the same: 1 latent frame in, 4 frames out, persistent cache
    (important for later when saying that within those 4 frames they bleed into each other)

\subsection{Architecture: Multi-view extension}

Input is [B,V,C,T,H,W]

\subsubsection{Design Decisions}

- adapt pretrained 3D VAE instead of training 4D VAE from scratch: transfer pretrained knowledge \cite{carreira2017quo, blattmann2023align}, data efficiency, keeping latent distribution close to the pretrained one
- new paths are zero-initialized, so model is exactly the pretrained per-view VAE at step 0

Encoder-side cross-view fusion at the bottleneck: per-view frozen Wan encoder stems, then fusion:

- fusion operates at 384 channels on a 8x downsampled grid -> tokens are cheap for attention, but early enough so that fused information shapes the latent
- fusion mechanism: cross-view attention: multi-head attention over all V*N tokens per time frame, RMSNorm, zero-init output projection, followed by binary tree merge (pairwise merge, each concat, through 2 ResBlocks)
- decode distinct views from one shared latent (view-conditioned decoding): single fused latent [16, T', H/8, W/8] is decoded V times by shared frozen decoder
    - view identity is injected as per-view latent LoRA adapters (Conv3d z->rank->z, zero-init up-projection)
- training: pre-fusion per-view encoder stem is frozen
- bottleneck + full decoder: LoRA rank 32, 0-init up-projections (limitation: no LoRA path exists for strided convolutions)
- new fusion modules fully trained
- strided temporal convolutions: frozen

% [added] training discipline is part of the method, not just the eval protocol
\subsubsection{Training discipline}

- overfit first, then generalize: every architectural change must first reconstruct a single sequence near-perfectly before it gets a full training run -> separates implementation/architecture failures from generalization behavior
- staged initialization (default): joint view+temporal model warm-starts from the converged temporal-compression checkpoint with re-randomized view attention -> model learns the temporal axis first, view axis second; joint training from Wan weights only is an ablation


\subsection{Objective}

- loss: nll = (L1 + 1.5*LPIPS)/exp(sigma) + sigma with learned scalar sigma, plus 1e-6 * KL, the standard \cite{rombach2022high, zheng2024opensora}
- optional adversarial term with three multi-view discriminator layouts (PatchGAN-style 3D \cite{isola2017image}):
    - flatten views into batch
    - stack views on channels
    - explicit 4D discriminator with a view axis and per-view embeddings


\subsection{Data and Preprocessing}

- NeRSemble \cite{kirschstein2023nersemble}:
    - 16 synchronized cameras, 73 fps, 3208x2200
    - redundant along two axes: time (adjacent frames differ only by smooth facial motion) and view (neighboring cameras observe same face under slightly varying perspective)
- this pipeline: select 8 frontal/upper-row cameras; temporal subsampling to 24 fps then uniform selection of T=9 frames
- per-camera color correction like provided in the dataset repo
- center square crop
- background removal with RobustVideoMatting \cite{lin2022robust} composited on white
- bilinear resize to {128, 256, 512}^2
- stored as [V, T, C, H, W] in [0,1], trained as [B, V, C, T, H, W] rescaled to [-1,1]
- data scales:
    - single sequence (overfitting) -> one person all expressions -> all participants with one expression -> all participants and all expressions
    - 10 held-out validation identities


\section{Experiments}

- training pipeline adapted from the Open-Sora wan finetuning (multi-view data loading, per-view gradient checkpointing); one L40S GPU per run

\subsection{Overview of planned experiments}

- core rate-quality matrix: \{per-view, fused latent\} x \{temporal compression off, on\}, plus pretrained Wan zero-shot as floor -> single-axis compression should degrade gracefully, does joint compression fall off the curve? (headline)
- fusion mechanism: cross-attention vs joint self-attention vs channel-concat Conv3d vs factorized 4D conv -> is the bottleneck the fusion design?
- view-conditioned decoding: per-view latent LoRA vs learned view embedding vs neither (negative control, expect ghosting)
- view count: V = 2, 4, 8 at fixed budget -> rate pressure grows with V
- unfreezing: LoRA-only vs unfrozen encoder vs everything unfrozen -> is it trainability or capacity?
- initialization: staged warm-start (default) vs joint from Wan weights
- loss: no discriminator vs the three multi-view discriminator layouts; perceptual/KL weight sensitivity; LoRA rank
- data scale: single sequence -> one person -> all people; artifacts should appear only when the model must generalize

All numbers come from reruns under one fixed protocol; per-experiment configs are pinned in the repository.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

- 2 stages:
    - overfit: train on single sequence, reconstruction must be near-perfect (gate: train PSNR at least 35 held for 3 consecutive epochs), verifies architecture
    - generalize: train on all participants (one expression) and evaluate on held-out identities -> encode rather than memorize
% [added] comparability spelled out, this is what makes the tables defensible
- comparability: budget is defined in optimizer updates, not epochs or wall clock; effective batch fixed at 64 (micro-batch x accumulation, re-balanced automatically on memory limits, reported if used) -> every variant sees the same number of updates AND the same samples; all evaluations fire at the same update steps; no variant is early-stopped or extended individually -> a plateau within the budget is the result, not a reason to train longer
- qualitative figures always show the same fixed participants (selected by dataset order, identical across all variants) -> grids directly comparable between models

\subsubsection{Dataset}

- train and evaluate on NeRSemble \cite{kirschstein2023nersemble} (refer to Sec. 3.5)
- V=2 synchronized frontal views, T=9 frames at 128x128, one expression sequence per participant
- why T=9 -> latent T'=3 = frame 0 + two 4-frame chunks, so both bleeding within chunks and artifacts across a chunk boundary are measurable (T=5 has no chunk boundary at all)
- why V=2 -> main setting uses the minimal view count -> any capacity failure can be traced back to the joint representation itself rather than to an aggressive compression ratio
    - also a clean 2x rate factor against the per-view references
    - larger view counts are studied as an explicit ablation axis later
- why 128x128 -> enables affordable controlled comparison of the full matrix on single GPUs; resolution is scaled separately as its own check
- hold out 10 participants entirely for validation (p018, p030, p038, p085, p097, p124, p175, p226, p227, p240)
- all reported metrics are computed on these unseen identities
- every experiment uses: AdamW (lr 5e-4, constant), bf16, effective batch 64, 170 epochs, LoRA rank 32, EMA 0.9999, no discriminator (UNLESS STATED OTHERWISE)

\subsubsection{Metrics}

- PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS \cite{zhang2018unreasonable} over validation set
- in addition: report 3 diagnostics which focus on localizing where information is lost:
    - (i) cross-view similarity = mean pairwise cosine similarity between reconstructed views, with the similarity of the ground-truth views as reference (values above the reference indicate view ghosting)
    - (ii) bleed ratio = the ratio of mean absolute inter-frame differences of the reconstruction to those of the ground truth, computed separately within 4-frame temporal chunks and across chunk boundaries (a value of 1 is faithful motion; values well below 1 indicate temporal bleeding)
      % [added] the raw curves behind the ratio
      - also plot the raw per-frame |delta| curves for GT vs recon: flat segments inside chunks make the bleeding directly visible
    - (iii) the per-frame-index error profile, which exposes cold-start and chunk-boundary artifacts

\subsubsection{Reference points}

- no like-for-like baseline exists (nothing else produces a joint multi-view latent) -> per-view models are reported as reference points, not baselines: pretrained Wan zero-shot, and per-view LoRA-finetuned Wan
- both use V times our latent rate and enforce no cross-view consistency -> all models are placed on a rate-quality plane instead of a single-rate ranking
- compression ratio: r = (V * 3 * T * H * W) / (V' * 16 * T' * (H/8) * (W/8)); our configs span per-view without TC up to fused + TC (todo: exact numbers per config)

\subsection{Main Results}

- a lot of tables and figures to see results

probably something like (current bullet points are based on prior experiments but since all will be repeated, it will probably change):
- joint compression collapses; single-axis compression is fine (the rate-quality curve is the headline figure)
- fusion mechanism barely matters (cross-attention, joint self-attention, 4D, ...) -> fusion mechanism is not the bottleneck
- view conditioning barely matters (learnable view embeddings vs per-view LoRAs)
- loss config very important: best with no discriminator or 4D multi-view discriminator, standard 3D PatchGAN worse colors and less sharp -> if a discriminator is used, it should see the view axis jointly
- lower perceptual and KL weights improved pixel fidelity
- LoRA rank ablation (expect a plateau)

\subsection{Analysis: Where does joint compression fail?}

- generalization failure: overfitting is clean, artifacts only when many identities are trained -> compressed representation can memorize temporal detail but can't encode it for new content
- chunk mechanism: decoding all latent frames in a single NON-CAUSAL pass removes frame-0 and boundary artifacts -> cost of the causal property, not of temporal compression per se
% [added] tie the diagnostics into the analysis
- diagnostics localize the failure: cross-view similarity of recons rises above the GT level (ghosting) while bleed ratio falls below 1 within chunks (temporal averaging) -> both are decoder-regresses-to-the-mean signatures

\subsection{Ablations}

\subsubsection{Fusion mechanism}

- joint self-attention: one attention over the concatenated token sequence of all views, then concat + 2 ResBlocks
- channel concat Conv3d: views stacked on channels, 1x1x1 Conv3d + GN/SiLU + two symmetric (non-causal) 3D residual blocks
- factorized 4D convolution: spatial 3x3 Conv2d, temporal 3x3x3 Conv3d, then a view-axis Conv3d with kernel (V,3,3) compressing V->1, a (2+1+1)D factorization

\subsubsection{View-conditioned decoding}

- as alternative to per-view latent LoRA adapters: view identity is injected as (i) a learned per-view latent embedding (nn.Embedding(V, 16) added to z)
% [added] the negative control makes "conditioning works" a measured claim
- negative control: no view conditioning at all -> expect full ghosting; anchors the cross-view similarity diagnostic

\subsubsection{Pre-fusion encoder with LoRAs}

- optionally add LoRAs to the frozen per-view encoder stem -> does adapting the encoder before fusion help?

\subsubsection{Unfreeze temporal convolutions}

- strided temporal convolutions allow no LoRA path, are frozen in all LoRA configurations -> unfreeze them (and optionally the full encoder or full decoder)
% [added] why this ablation matters
- if even full unfreezing does not fix joint compression, the bottleneck is capacity, not trainability

- V = 2, 4, 8 at fixed budget and fixed fusion mechanism -> rate pressure on the shared latent grows with V, expect monotone degradation and earlier onset of ghosting

\subsubsection{Initialization strategy}

- warm-starting the joint model from a temporal-compression checkpoint with re-randomized view-attention OR joint training from Wan-pretrained weights only

\section{Discussion}

This section discusses the main findings and considers implications and potential applications of this work.

\subsection{Capacity}

- in our setup:
    - Wan latent has 16 channels per 8x8x4 pixel block: 3*8*8*4/16 = 48x compression
    - fusing V=2 views into one latent doubles this to 96x; V=4 to 192x
- for comparison:
    - Emu \cite{dai2023emu}: image latents needed a widening from 4 to 16 channels just to hold fine detail at 48x-equivalent rates
    - LTX-Video \cite{hacohen2024ltx}: 192x compression uses 128 channels
- our setting demands ~2-4x the information density of the pretrained latent at fixed width -> the observed failure is exactly what rate exhaustion predicts: the decoder regresses to the conditional mean along whichever axis was compressed, showing up as ghosting (view mean) and bleeding (temporal mean)
- two further observations supporting this (todo: check first if this still holds in the repeated experiments!): (i) the choice of fusion mechanism and view-conditioning mechanism barely affected quality, and (ii) what fails first under joint compression is high-frequency identity detail (nuanced facial appearance, small accessories such as earrings) -> precisely the detail class that widening image latents from 4 to 16 channels was needed to preserve in Emu \cite{dai2023emu}

\subsection{Bottleneck: Encoder-side latent}

- decoder side works: per-view LoRA + embeddings reliably disambiguate views from a shared latent
- conditioning mechanism insensitivity
- information is missing, not mis-decoded: encoder-side latent would need to hold more than it can
-> decoder can emit distinct views but the fixed-width latent can't carry them under joint compression
- post-hoc fixes, eg embeddings, residual decoders, and auxiliary losses, can't recover information -> everything which operates after the bottleneck failed

\subsection{Implications and Applications}

designing a 4D tokenizer:
- might require scaling latent width with the number of compressed axes (\cite{chen2024deep, hacohen2024ltx} suggest roughly proportional scaling) -> ie a 4D VAE wants 32-64 channels, which requires pretraining or heavy finetuning, not adapters
- keep view identity out of the latent: compress only shared content; carry view as decoder conditioning (our per-view LoRA is the adapter-scale version; a real system would train it)
- asymmetric: compress time natively, keep views uncompressed but consistent via cross-view attention -> matching what 4D diffusion systems converged to \cite{xie2024sv4d, shao2024human4dit}


\section{Conclusion}

- beginning sentence first
- extended a pretrained 3D video VAE to multi-view video data, zero-initialized cross-view fusion and per-view low-rank decoding -> preserving the pretrained latent distribution and unconditional sampling
- study on multi-view facial video: shows the fixed 16-channel latent absorbs either 4x temporal compression or the fused view axis, but not both -> joint compression exhausts the latent's capacity and the decoder regresses to means -> resulting in cross-view ghosting and intra-chunk temporal bleeding
- future 4D tokenizers: scale latent capacity with the number of compressed axes or keep view identity out of the latent


\section{Limitations}

- compressed data: 9-frame clips (rate pressure grows with T -> longer clips could shift conclusions), 2-8 frontal views, not the full rig
- one backbone family (Wan 2.1) tried; the chunked-cache specifics of the temporal path are Wan's, other causal video VAEs may fail differently
% [added]
- adapter-scale training budget: conclusions hold for LoRA-based adaptation of a frozen backbone; full pretraining at 4D scale could move the capacity wall (that experiment is exactly what this study argues one should budget for)
- downstream generation untested: we evaluate the latent by reconstruction; whether a diffusion model samples well from the fused latent is future work
- single domain (faces, studio capture, white background); redundancy structure of in-the-wild multi-view data may differ
